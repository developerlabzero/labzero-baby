    About this prototype

    LabZero Baby is an open-source simulation for training nurses, midwives, doctors and pharmacists to care for newborns and infants: spotting the sick baby, routine well-baby care, immunisation catch-up and the mother-baby pair. It runs in a browser and works offline.

    The next chapter of LabZero Mama. It uses the same clinic, Ikhwezi Community Health Centre, and the same family. Lwazi, the 31-week baby born to Thembeka Ngcobo in LabZero Mama, comes home and grows up across the cases (“Lwazi's first year”), and other families from the same clinic join the queue.

    This pilot build has four cases, set for the first pilot at Sefako Makgatho Health Sciences University (SMU): a sick small baby after discharge, the six-week visit, a missed visit with a new HIV result while breastfeeding, and a zero-dose baby in a measles outbreak. Immunisation follows the South African public-sector schedule (EPI-SA). Educators can open any case directly from the lobby (“Educator: open a case directly”).

    Safety by design. All patients are fictional. The caregiver can only say lines from the approved case script. The dialogue layer only chooses which approved line answers a question; scoring rules, not AI, decide whether an action was safe. Learner records stay on this device.

    Tuto, your tutor

    Choose a mode in the lobby. Learn with Tuto gives a briefing at every step, hints on request (a nudge, a bigger hint, then the answer) and a review after each step. Practice keeps the hints, but each costs 5 points. Assessment switches Tuto off. Tuto teaches only from the approved case content: the same reasons and explanations the scoring uses.

    Patient dialogue

    The language model receives only the trainee's question and the list of approved question topics. It never sees learner records and never writes the patient's words.
